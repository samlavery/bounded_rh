                                                      Abstract
             We develop two complementary results for a method using a 25-term analytic window, clipped
          Gram traces and position pressures to count simple zeros on the critical line. A three-window
          compatibility inequality improves our preceding computer-assisted lower bound to

                            Nsc (T )           54923339574680150762799
                       lim inf       ≥ rnew :=                         > 0.6734765075.
                       T →∞ N (T )             81551975398437500000000
         The coefficients of the analytic window and the nine original local functionals are unchanged.
         Separately, a density-balanced mixture of two periodic point configurations proves that every
         certificate in an explicitly defined 25-mode affine-pressure clipped-Gram class lies below 0.674034,
         irrespective of block size or the local proof used to establish its block inequality. This is an upper
         bound on that proof interface, not on the actual proportion of zeta zeros, and is not asserted to
         be its sharp supremum. Both finite computational obligations have exact or outward interval
         certificates. The full inherited analytic argument is appended, with its nine local computations
         replayed in this work.

Lemma 5.1 (Mixture obstruction). Let a finite mixture of periodic gap configurations have mean
gap d = 1/r and mean full pair energy Ef , with r > 0. If (2) holds and its value satisfies κ ≥ r, then

                                        H(f ) − r(1 − Ef ) ≥ 0.                                    (14)

Proof. For t ≥ 0, Φ(t) ≤ (t − 1)2 . Average the universal block inequality over all cyclic starting
phases of one periodic word. The pressure average is P d; the finite block Frobenius sum is at most
mEf , since W ≥ 0 and omitted pair distances have nonnegative contribution. Taking the mixture
gives R ≤ mEf + P/r. On the other hand, m − R > 0 and κ ≥ r imply m(H(f ) − r) + rR − P ≥ 0.

averaging device for inequalities valid on every configuration; it need not represent the actual zeta
zero process.

7    What the method boundary does and does not say
The number 0.67514250966 . . . in teal-sea’s family-wall analysis [3, 4] bounds a specified uniform-
pressure/cap formula. Its stated large-n limit is H0 = 0.672500703679 . . .; the reported upper
estimate is not identified with an attained sharp supremum. It therefore cannot be substituted for
the limit of our joint-pressure, 25-mode clipped-Gram construction. Our new cap is derived directly
for the latter interface.
    Likewise, the quadratic window cost in (5) is not a theorem that successive optimization gains
decay geometrically. Such a rate would require additional control of the optimized coefficients, the
local constraints and the active projection geometry. No runtime extrapolation is used in either
theorem here.
    Longer compatible windows can increase the certified aggregate while retaining the same window.
Our numerical lengths 4 through 12 give candidate local values, without certified enclosures or
completed universal proofs. A tested interpolation with positive seventh-band weight also produced
a new low basin and no accepted improvement; its discovery results are kept as such. None of
these routes escapes Theorem 1.2 if it still yields (2). To exceed that cap one must leave at least
one defining condition of the class, for example the fixed 25-mode window space or the affine
pressure/clipped-trace transport. That observation does not prove that a particular enlarged class
will improve the lower bound.
    The Navigator’s common-realization and inheritance rules have a concrete role here: overlapping
windows share variables; local and asymptotic claims are typed separately; numerical minima cannot
become lower certificates; and an upper bound on a proof interface cannot become an upper bound
on the zeros. SP and complexity perspectives motivate the overlap and certificate design, but supply
no unproved mathematical premise.

Lemma 3.1 (Known clipped remainder). Let P = V V ∗ ⪰ 0 have s unit columns and let Q be

The certificate separates discovery from verification. Numerical minimization and linear programming
proposed the weights and the prices. Their apparent minima are not mathematical input. The proof
inputs are the explicit rationals, the exhaustive local interval checks, the exact vertex checks, and
the written analytic lemmas. The accepted base target is 0.0078647405. At the exact rational point

    The present search removed the preceding artificial pair-weight trust region and varied the 16
original cosine coefficients jointly with pair and pressure weights. It then extended the window to 24
nonconstant cosine modes. The expanded window was frozen before optimizing the auxiliary local
inequalities. Their scales were evaluated together in the exact pressure relaxation. Sampled minima
and optimizer success flags did not decide acceptance: every retained inequality had to pass the
exhaustive interval checker. The archive preserves numerical failures, superseded designs, and all
target choices.
    The C++ verifier is adapted from AMTOPA under its MIT license. The changes use a fixed
Loewner Hessian minorant, enclose rational centers directly, round compound expressions step by
step, and add the proved quadratic terminal bound. The finite minorant must be distinguished from
an entrywise half-infinite Hessian enclosure; an interval LDL test of the latter need not terminate
successfully. The 192-bit Arb table builder is a new implementation.
    The full replay uses Python 3, python-flint 0.9.0, and a C++17 compiler with little-endian IEEE
binary64, extended-precision long double, round-to-nearest mode, and no fused contraction. It
checks all input identities, regenerates and hashes the tables, recompiles, and executes all nine local

archive.
    The Navigator’s shared-realization requirement motivated retaining the actual nine pressures
in one polyhedron instead of combining incompatible scalar summaries. This is a concrete use
